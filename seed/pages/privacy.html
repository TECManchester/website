Who we are

For data protection purposes we are the "controller" of the information described here — that means we decide what is collected and why. If you have any question about this notice, or want to make a request about your information, email us at {contact.email}.

What we collect, and why

We collect the information you choose to give us through the forms on this site. If you allow it, we also use Google Analytics to understand how the site is used (see Cookies below). We don't advertise, we don't sell your information, and we don't build profiles of you.

When you contact us

The contact form asks for your name and email address, and optionally a phone number and subject. We use it to answer you. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests — you have asked us a question and we need your details to reply.

When you send a prayer request

You can send a prayer request anonymously. If you give your name or contact details, they are stored with the request. A prayer request may reveal things about your religious beliefs or your health, which the law treats as special category data and protects more strictly.

Our lawful basis is consent, and the additional condition we rely on for special category data is your explicit consent, given when you submit the form. You can withdraw it at any time by emailing us, and we will delete the request.

Prayer requests are treated as pastoral confidences. They are sent to a separate prayer inbox rather than the church's general email, and on the website only the small number of people trusted with pastoral confidences can read them. If you tick the box to share a request with the wider prayer team, we take that as your permission to pass it beyond the immediate pastoral team; if you don't, we won't.

When you make a Gift Aid declaration

To claim Gift Aid we are required by HMRC to record your title, full first name and surname, your home address including house name or number, and your postcode. We also record the wording of the declaration you agreed to and when you made it. Email and phone are optional and used only to confirm the declaration or contact you about changes.

Our lawful basis is legal obligation — we cannot make a valid Gift Aid claim without these details. This information is shared with HM Revenue & Customs when we make a claim.

When you plan a visit

The Plan a Visit form asks for your name, email and phone number, the {service.day} you plan to come, how many adults and children are coming, your children's ages if you'd like us to be ready for them, and anything else you'd like us to know. We use it to welcome you, and we email you a confirmation with the time and address. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests — you have asked us to expect you. Please don't include children's names.

When you fill in a connect card

The connect card asks for your name, contact details, postcode and how you'd like to get involved, so our welcome team can follow up. We ask only for your postcode, not your full address. Our lawful basis is consent, which you can withdraw at any time.

When you ask to join a Connect Group

We pass your name, contact details and message to the leader of the group you chose, and to our welcome team, so they can get in touch. Group leaders' email addresses are never shown on the site. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests — you have asked to be put in touch.

When you sign up for the G-Squad or Alpha

We store the details you give so the team leader or Alpha host can contact you and arrange your place. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests.

When you join the mailing list

We store your email address to send you church news. Our lawful basis is consent. Email us at any time and we will remove your address.

When you have a website account

If you help run the website, we store your name, email address, your role, and the changes you make — WordPress keeps earlier versions of pages so mistakes can be undone. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests in running the site securely.

How long we keep it

  • Gift Aid declarations — for six years after the last gift they cover, because HMRC requires it. A cancelled declaration is kept for the same period as evidence of when it applied.
  • Prayer requests — only while they are being prayed for and followed up, then deleted. Ask us sooner and we will delete them straight away.
  • Contact messages — up to 12 months after we have dealt with your enquiry.
  • Visit plans — up to 12 months after the date you planned to visit.
  • Connect cards, Connect Group requests, and G-Squad and Alpha sign-ups — up to two years, or until you ask us to delete them.
  • Mailing list — until you ask to be removed.
  • Website accounts — while the account is active; page history is kept afterwards so the record of changes stays intact.

Who else is involved

We do not sell your information and we do not share it for marketing. We use a small number of suppliers to run the site, who process information on our instructions:

  • The Elevation Church, our parent church — hosts this website and its database on a server it runs with Liquid Web. Form submissions and website accounts are stored there.
  • Resend — sends the site's emails, such as form notifications and visit confirmations.
  • Google — Google Analytics, only if you allow analytics; and the embedded Google Maps and YouTube players, only if you allow them or press play.
  • Podbean — the podcast player on our Resources page, only if you allow it or press play.
  • HM Revenue & Customs — receives Gift Aid claims.

Some of these suppliers are based outside the UK. Where information is transferred abroad, it is protected by the safeguards UK data protection law requires, such as the International Data Transfer Agreement or an adequacy decision.

Cookies and similar technology

We ask before anything optional loads. On your first visit a small banner lets you accept or reject analytics and embedded maps and videos, or choose each one. Nothing from Google or Podbean loads until you decide.

  • Your privacy choice — kept in your browser's local storage, not a cookie, so we don't have to ask again. It never leaves your device.
  • Google Analytics cookies — only if you allow analytics. Google Analytics 4 sets _ga and _ga_<id> cookies, which last up to two years, to count visits and see which pages help people. Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses. If you later turn analytics off, we stop loading it and delete these cookies.
  • Dismissed announcements — if you close a pop-up notice, we remember that in local storage so it doesn't reappear straight away.
  • Login cookies — only for people who help run the website, so WordPress knows it is them. They are essential and can't be turned off while you are signed in.

Embedded maps, videos and the podcast player come from Google (Google Maps and YouTube) and Podbean. Loading one tells that company your IP address and details about your browser, and it may set its own cookies. Unless you have allowed maps and videos, you'll see a placeholder with a button instead, and nothing is requested until you press it.

Your rights

You have the right to:

  • ask for a copy of the information we hold about you;
  • ask us to correct anything that is wrong;
  • ask us to delete it, where we don't have a legal reason to keep it;
  • ask us to restrict how we use it, or object to our using it;
  • ask us to transfer it to you or another organisation, where it was given with your consent;
  • withdraw consent at any time, where consent is what we relied on.

To exercise any of these, email {contact.email}. We will respond within one month. There is no charge.

If you're unhappy

Please tell us first — we would rather put it right. If you are still not satisfied, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK regulator for data protection, at ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint or on 0303 123 1113.

Changes to this notice

If we change how we use your information we will update this page and the date at the top. If the change is significant we will say so clearly on the site.
